# Naming Research

## RecipeLab

Status: promising wording, likely risky as a brand.

Why it fits:

- Clear connection to recipe iteration and experimentation.
- Works for schools, technical cards, recipe versions, and RecipeHub direction.
- Sounds productized without being too narrow.

Known signals to watch:

- `recipelab.co` exists and appears to be a recipe/versioning/collaboration
  product for chefs and commercial kitchens.
- A `RECIPELAB` trademark application appeared in 2026 for downloadable mobile
  applications related to recipes, meal planning, nutrition guidance, and food
  recommendations.
- The product/category overlap is close enough that this name should not be
  used without proper legal/trademark review.

Decision for now:

- Keep as competitor and naming research.
- Track product maturity and positioning.
- Do not commit to `RecipeLab` as the project name.

## Workbench Direction

Workbench is a strong naming direction because the product is not just a recipe
book. It is a place to calculate, test, compare, version, export, and teach
recipes.

Pros:

- Technical and professional.
- Suggests making, testing, and refining.
- Works for calculators and RecipeHub.
- Less generic than `recipe`.
- Can fit B2B/school positioning.

Cons:

- Slightly less immediately food-specific.
- May need a food/culinary modifier.
- Domain availability will matter a lot.

## Chef Lab

Status: sounds good, but appears crowded/generic.

Why it fits:

- Immediately culinary.
- Friendly and understandable.
- Suggests experimentation and learning.
- Works for schools and beginner-facing products.

Known signals to watch:

- `CHEF LAB` had a US trademark registration by Cable News Network, Inc. for
  cooking/entertainment media. It appears cancelled/dead, but it is still a
  signal that the phrase has been used commercially:
  https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/86664279/CHEF-LAB
- `chef-lab.it` exists as a professional culinary ingredient/product line:
  https://www.chef-lab.it/en/
- `le-chef-lab.com` exists as a recipe generator:
  https://le-chef-lab.com/
- There are additional ChefLab/Chef Lab uses around AI recipe apps, thermometers,
  shared kitchen spaces, and food businesses.
- A Spanish `CHEF LAB` mark appears associated with Gourmet Food Solutions SL:
  https://www.empresia.es/distintivo/chef-lab/oepm/m-4252788/

Decision for now:

- Keep as research.
- Do not commit to `Chef Lab` without proper trademark/domain review.
- Consider more distinctive variants if this direction remains appealing.

## MiseBench

`MiseBench` comes from `mise en place`, the professional kitchen idea of having
everything prepared, organized, and ready before service.

This phrase remains important for `MiseLab` even though `MiseBench` was not
selected. See `docs/BRAND_NOTES.md`.

Why it fits:

- Has a chef/pro-kitchen feel.
- Suggests organization, preparation, precision, and workflow.
- Works nicely with a "bench/workbench" metaphor.
- Could support recipes, ingredients, prep, production sheets, and teaching.

Risks:

- Less obvious for non-chefs and students who have not heard `mise en place`.
- May require brand education.
- The spelling/pronunciation can be unclear internationally.

Decision for now:

- Interesting, distinctive, chef-coded option.
- Better as a brand direction than an immediately descriptive product name.
- Worth comparing against clearer names like `FormulaBench` and
  `CulinaryBench`.
